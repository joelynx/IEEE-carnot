# Critique of the Task 1 Working Notes

Reviewed 2 November 2026. Scope: `main.tex`, its tables, the standalone source and PDF, and the supporting evaluation records and implementation. The manuscript and experimental records were not edited. This review distinguishes weaknesses already present in the October draft from information published after it was built.

The paper has a useful core: a carefully instrumented program-of-thought system, strong development results, concrete convention-reading failures, and unusually candid disclosures. Its main weaknesses are the outdated results framing, a statistical safeguard described more strongly than its definition supports, and conclusions that extend beyond the evaluation design. I would revise these before submission.

## 1. High: the central results framing is now outdated

**Evidence:** `main.tex:30`, `:49`, `:125`, and `:185` say test scores are unknown and hidden-template performance is unmeasured. That was appropriate for the PDF built on 25 October. The organisers published results on 28 October. Their current table lists carnot at rank **17 of 27**, combined FAC **0.985304**, hidden FAC **0.976025**, combined checkpoint **0.988745**, and **917/928** correct finals. The page also discloses one wording/reference inconsistency and says the published scores retain the frozen evaluation. [Official test results](https://the-finai.github.io/IEEE-bigdata-cup/task1/leaderboard/test/), [machine-readable results](https://the-finai.github.io/IEEE-bigdata-cup/task1/results/2026-test-results.json).

**Why it matters:** This changes the main scientific question. The paper can now discuss the gap between development ceiling performance and measured hidden-template performance. It should no longer end with generalisation entirely unknown. Public scores still cannot establish whether R1, R3, or R4 improved hidden performance relative to R0; that requires unavailable comparative test scores. Nor do aggregate results identify the causes of individual test errors.

**Revision:** Add an explicitly dated post-results update, identify the evaluated system against the submission record, and rewrite the abstract, results, and conclusion around development performance plus the observed test result. Preserve the distinction between pre-results decisions and later observations. Include the organiser's evaluation caveat without attributing carnot's errors to that question.

## 2. High: the original “non-inferiority” rule does not establish non-inferiority

**Evidence:** `main.tex:108` defines the gate as a point difference at least `-2*sigma_run` and the interval's **upper** endpoint at least zero. The implementation agrees: `carnot/compare.py:128`. R2 passes despite its H-mode checkpoint interval being approximately `[-0.006667, +0.004762]`, much wider than the stated tolerance of `0.000962` (`main.tex:122`).

**Why it matters:** An interval containing zero can also contain substantial harm. Failure to detect inferiority is insufficient to demonstrate non-inferiority. For a prespecified acceptable loss `m`, a confidence-bound approach needs a suitable **lower** bound above `-m`. The historical rule is an operational adoption rule with a point-estimate tolerance; its name currently gives it stronger inferential meaning.

**Revision:** Preserve the original decisions and label their criterion accurately, for example “no detected degradation plus a point-estimate tolerance.” State that it did not establish statistical non-inferiority. Describe the later continuation's lower-bound rule separately. Do not retrospectively substitute a different rule and present it as the preregistered one.

The margin also comes from only three R0 replicates. It measures one configuration's observed variability, rather than a justified maximum acceptable performance loss across all later configurations.

## 3. High: H-mode provides very little additional evidence about hidden templates

**Evidence:** `main.tex:99–102` says all templates appear in every split and H-mode replaces a worked example only when it shares the evaluated template. The continuation's `regression/REPORT.md` makes the overlap explicit: only **6 of 580 cases** have different prompts, and **2,870 of 2,900 generator requests** are shared between S and H.

**Why it matters:** H-mode removes a particular source of example overlap; it does not hold whole templates out of development, prompt design, or convention analysis. Agreement in both modes is therefore a highly overlapping sensitivity check. Calling it “hidden simulation” encourages readers to infer a much broader distribution shift than was tested. The manuscript's own public-input analysis also shows actual novel-route questions have different slot identifiers, rounding patterns, and lengths.

**Revision:** Rename H-mode to describe exemplar-template exclusion, quantify the overlap, and explicitly say the two modes are not independent replications. Present forced-novel evaluation as a regression check on familiar templates. A genuinely held-out template experiment would need separation before method development; this cannot be manufactured retrospectively from the same development history.

## 4. Medium: the later safeguard conflates different estimands and obscures its chronology

**Evidence:** `main.tex:180` introduces “our non-inferiority margin of 0.001” without first distinguishing it from the earlier `2*sigma_run` rule. It then says a zero-difference result needs about 2,995 independent templates to be certified. The supporting `experiments/improvement_continuation_20261021/statistical_feasibility.md` is more precise: the zero-event bound concerns the probability that an independent template contains a regression, **not an interval for weighted FAC loss**.

**Why it matters:** A bound on the fraction of templates with any observed loss, an official hierarchically weighted score difference, and model sampling variability are separate quantities. Moving from a score margin to a template-event bound changes the claim being certified. The number 2,995 follows from `1 - 0.05^(1/n) < 0.001`; it is not a universal sample-size requirement for weighted FAC non-inferiority.

**Revision:** Explain the three historical stages: the original operational adoption gate, the continuation's lower-bound/zero-event policy, and S2's count-based safeguard. Name each estimand and its assumptions. Keep the conclusion “no observed final-answer harm on these familiar templates.”

Also correct “R4's own sigma_run is 0.000962”: `main.tex:105` and `paper/TABLES.md:44` attribute that estimate to **R0**. The S2 report observes zero final disagreement between its two fresh R4 samples; that is a different observation.

## 5. Medium: the abstract contains factual contradictions

All occur at `main.tex:30` and in the compiled PDF.

- **“Half of the test questions” are hidden:** the draft itself gives 348 hidden questions out of 928, which is **37.5%**. Hidden questions receive half of the score weight.
- **“No final-answer difference ... on 48 new ... families”:** Table VI and `experiments/improvement_continuation_20261021/ablations/WAVE1_ASSESSMENT.md` report R4 at **95/96**, versus **96/96** for the variants, in the first 24 families. All tie in the second 24. “No confirmed general improvement” would match the evidence; “no difference” does not.
- **“Gains appeared only on underspecified wording”:** the same wave-1 report describes R4 misapplying a stated annual-to-quarterly conversion. The advantage is small and unconfirmed, but it is not exclusively an ambiguity effect.
- **“Every candidate ... adopted under a non-inferiority rule”:** several candidates were rejected or never promoted, and R4 used a distinct development-leaderboard rule. Summarise the actual decision process without treating it as one universal adoption rule.

These are especially consequential because the abstract is the most widely read part of the paper.

## 6. Medium: the evidence supports reliability observations more strongly than causal claims

**Evidence:** `main.tex:40`, `:44–46`, and `:187` say computation and formatting errors are removed by construction and imply the system design puts seen-template performance at ceiling. The 8,888-field round trip tests rendering of already correct gold values. It does not test whether generated programs calculate the right quantities. The sandbox also uses finite-precision decimal approximations for irrational functions (`main.tex:79`).

**Why it matters:** Exact arithmetic helps execute a chosen calculation faithfully; it does not prevent incorrect formulas, branching, parameter extraction, or intermediate rounding choices. The paper presents no isolated comparison attributing the final score to exact arithmetic, the formatter, examples, and sampling separately. R0 versus R1 changes both effort and sample count, so that comparison cannot isolate self-consistency's effect either.

**Revision:** Say that the inspected residual tuning errors were convention-related and that no arithmetic-execution or formatting errors were observed there. Describe the round trip as formatter validation. Present the contribution as an integrated engineering system unless controlled component evidence is available. No new model runs are necessary to calibrate the prose.

One concrete implementation mismatch should also be fixed: `main.tex:82` says a float causes a rendering error, but `carnot/formatter.py:100–102` converts a float to a Decimal, which a half-up field can accept. Exact fields reject it. State the narrower behavior accurately.

## 7. Medium: the synthetic results do not justify a general explanation of financial reasoning difficulty

**Evidence:** `main.tex:133` and `:182` generalise from explicitly worded generated problems to the claim that candidates help only with underspecification. The study stopped at a development ceiling and never produced sealed confirmation evidence. The manuscript correctly acknowledges that authoring and checking share a model family (`main.tex:185`).

**Why it matters:** Multiple checking procedures are useful validation, but agreement among related models does not establish an independent semantic oracle. If neutral blind-solver agreement is a condition for admitting a family, the resulting panel is also conditioned on solvability by that checking procedure. It is a useful validated panel, but a selective basis for claims about what kinds of tasks are intrinsically hard.

**Revision:** Conclude that **this generation and validation process did not yield a discriminating confirmation set**. Discuss ambiguity as one observed mechanism, alongside the stated units-conversion failure. Avoid claiming that fully specified financial reasoning is generally solved or that remaining model gains must come from ambiguity.

## 8. Medium: per-field voting does not guarantee a coherent executed chain

**Evidence:** `main.tex:85` and `carnot/solver.py:197–219` choose each field independently. Different winning fields may come from different sampled programs.

**Why it matters:** Every field can have an execution provenance while the assembled row has no single program that produces it. That is compatible with the official fieldwise scoring, but narrower than a guarantee of a verified financial chain. The later complete-row selector addresses a different property and is not part of accepted R4.

**Revision:** State this limitation explicitly. If existing logs already report whether a voted row occurs as a complete candidate, that would be a useful descriptive diagnostic. Do not imply that passing the schema validator verifies mathematical relationships between slots.

## Presentation and revision priorities

The draft currently reads partly as an audit record. `main.tex:122` packs almost the entire decision table back into one long paragraph, including fractions, both intervals, thresholds, and exceptions. The abstract is also overloaded. Detailed split counts, submission identifiers, incidents, and repeated ceiling statements compete with the scientific result.

Keep the exact-program pipeline, the development and test results, two or three convention examples, the honest selection disclosures, and the synthetic-panel limitation. Shorten the decision narrative to the decision and its evidential strength; let the table carry the numbers. Compress resource accounting and operational history, with links to the supporting record if it will be shared. Use the recovered space for the public test outcome and the limits of the validation design.

A defensible revised thesis is: **The system achieves near-perfect development performance with reliable program execution and specification-based rendering, while the published hidden-template result exposes a generalisation gap that the familiar-template checks and synthetic panels did not resolve.** Aggregate test results do not establish which component caused that gap.
